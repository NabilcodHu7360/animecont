**PLUNDERER — HOW IT ENDS: THE FAKE, THE ABYSS, AND THE WORLD WITHOUT NUMBERS**

[MUSIC CUE: a lone piano over low static, like a number ticking down]

[HOST] The anime left you in a world ruled by Counts — where every number etched on your body decides your worth, and dropping to zero drops you into the Abyss. What the manga does next is tear the whole system down: a villain hiding for three hundred years, a father who climbed out of hell, and a choice about whether a world built on numbers deserves to keep existing.

[VISUAL: a floating sky-island above a black chasm, a single glowing number burning on a hand]

## ACT ONE — THE FAKE SCHMELMAN

The man everyone fears — "Schmelman Bach," the tyrant running the Special Service — is a lie. [ch.30-53] He's a clone. The real villain is **Firenda**, a three-hundred-year-old scientist who has spent the entire story disguised as a lowly soldier named Nikola, able to steal anyone's appearance and abilities with a touch. [ch.30-53] [VISUAL: a tally of scratches covering an entire wall, floor to ceiling]

Her Count — the number of times she has stolen — is enormous. And she has kept the *real* Schmelman locked in a pocket dimension called the Ende, torturing him psychologically for three centuries. [ch.30-53]

[VISUAL: a laboratory of glass cylinders lit from within, one empty, centuries of dust on the floor]

[BEAT]

[VISUAL: a cracked porcelain mask resting on dark cloth, lit from one side]

She isn't the only one wearing a mask. Pele Poporo — the short comic-relief soldier — is secretly **Gespenst Zerlegen, the Piercing Baron**, a Colonel General who reads minds and has been quietly watching everyone. [ch.30-53] Beneath the act, he genuinely cares for Lynn. [ch.30-53]

---

## ACT TWO — THE MAN FROM THE ABYSS

[MUSIC CUE: rain, and a low cello]

[HOST] And out of the Abyss itself climbs a ghost.

**Sakai Tokikaze** — Licht's adoptive brother, Hina's father — survived three hundred years in the pit below the world. [ch.30-53] [VISUAL: seven ornate ballots on a stone table, four of them claimed, lit from a single overhead shaft]

He's collected four of the seven Ballots, a voting majority in the Althing, and he uses it coldly: cutting Althea's food supply, annulling the nuclear ban, declaring outright war between the Abyss and the sky. [ch.30-53] His goal is to force the people above to finally *feel* the suffering of the people below.

[VISUAL: looking up from the floor of a vast chasm, a distant island blotting out the sun] He kidnaps his own daughter Hina, and his blade can now cut through dimensions themselves. [ch.30-53]

[VISUAL: two men facing each other in torrential rain, one weeping, one already turning away]

Licht and Tokikaze reunite in the rain. [ch.30-53] Licht cries and apologizes for nearly killing him three hundred years ago. Tokikaze embraces him — then draws his sword and walks away. [ch.30-53] Their dreams can't coexist. War is coming.

## ACT THREE — THE LAST WAR BEGINS

[INTENSITY: high]

[MUSIC CUE: percussion enters, martial and grim]

[HOST] Firenda ends her own puppet without ceremony — killing the clone Schmelman as he dies calling her "Mama." [ch.54-70] Hina slaps her across the face. [ch.54-70] Then Firenda drops the Nikola disguise for good and reveals the horror she's become: she has plundered the powers of all seven Legendary Aces — Schmelman's ice, Jail's iron, Tokikaze's speed, Alan's fire. [ch.54-70] She is, by every measure, the most powerful person alive.

[VISUAL: a long shadow thrown across a marble floor, far larger than whatever casts it]

[VISUAL: seven elemental auras — ice, iron, fire, lightning — swirling around a single silhouetted figure]

[BEAT]

She weaponizes the people around Licht against him — injecting Tokikaze with a drug that forces his bloodlust gene active, turning the father into a berserk weapon aimed at his own brother. [ch.54-70] [VISUAL: a syringe empty on a metal tray, light glinting off the needle]

Only Eins, a Class A clone who loves Tokikaze, fights the drug's control from within. [ch.54-70]

[VISUAL: a frozen classroom, untouched for centuries, a man reading a picture book to empty chairs]

And in the Ende, the group finds the **real Schmelman** at last — preserved and frozen, looking like Japan three hundred years ago, sitting in a classroom reading a picture book. [ch.54-70] [VISUAL: rows of small empty desks under a thick layer of frost, one book open]

His old Class A students take their seats and ask him to read to them. He cries. And he does. [ch.54-70]

## ACT FOUR — MASTERING THE VIOLENCE

[INTENSITY: high]

[MUSIC CUE: the piano returns, building]

[HOST] Firenda makes her last stand — freezing Licht solid, beating Jail with his own iron. [ch.54-70] But Licht breaks free by doing what no one else could: instead of *fighting* Schmelman's bloodlust genes, he fuses them into his own Count and masters the violence rather than being ruled by it. His hair turns black. [ch.54-70]

[VISUAL: a figure encased in ice, hairline cracks spreading outward from the chest, light bleeding through]

Then comes the line that defines the whole series. **Mizuka shoots Firenda** and tells her that a heartless person can never defeat Licht — because he has something she can never steal. [ch.54-70] Love. Connection. The people who believe in him. It cannot be plundered. [ch.54-70] [VISUAL: a shattered mirror scattering hundreds of stolen reflections across stone]

Tokikaze, freed, delivers the killing blow. [ch.54-70] Firenda — who spent three centuries stealing everything — dies with nothing. [ch.54-70]

[VISUAL: an empty throne room, a crown on the floor, dust settling in a shaft of cold light]

## ACT FIVE — THE WORLD WITHOUT NUMBERS

[INTENSITY: normal]

[MUSIC CUE: everything softens — strings, hopeful and aching]

[HOST] Killing the villain isn't the ending. The real enemy was always the system.

The only true solution is to destroy the Althing entirely — no Althing means no Counts, no Abyss, no hierarchy ever again. [ch.71-82] [VISUAL: an immense clockwork mechanism grinding to a halt, gears frozen mid-turn]

But someone has to remain inside the Ende to sustain the dismantling. **Schmelman volunteers** — saying he designed this world, so he will be the one to end it. [VISUAL: a single figure alone in an endless white void, back turned, walking away from a closing door] [ch.71-82] Class A protests. He says goodbye. And the sky island Althea begins to descend. [ch.71-82]

[VISUAL: a whole island lowering from the clouds, children below looking up into direct sunlight for the first time]

Althea comes down slowly, gently — settling, not crashing. [ch.71-82] [VISUAL: small hands raised toward a sky they have never seen]

Abyss children who lived their whole lives in darkness feel real sunlight for the first time. [ch.71-82] Two worlds, severed for three hundred years, begin to reunite. [ch.71-82]

## CLOSING — A WORLD OF PEACE, HAPPINESS, AND BORING

[INTENSITY: low]

[MUSIC CUE: warm, unhurried, a little playful]

[HOST] Three years later, in New Calendar Year 3, the world is so peaceful it's almost dull — and the former soldiers who complain about it are quietly, deeply relieved. [ch.82-82] Gespenst asks Lynn to build a family properly, and she couldn't be happier. [ch.82-82] Class A clones rebuild the former Abyss shoulder to shoulder with their originals.

[VISUAL: scaffolding over a half-built street, warm dust in low sunlight] [ch.82-82]

[VISUAL: a quiet town square at golden hour, market stalls, children running, no walls anywhere]

Licht and Jail finally have their promised rematch — no winner shown. [ch.82-82] [VISUAL: two practice blades resting against a fence post at dusk]

Jail asks why Licht only ever fights him at full strength. Licht answers that Jail is his best rival, and there's no one else he'd insult by holding back. [ch.82-82]

[VISUAL: a baby held up to the light, a bare wrist with no number on it at all]

[VISUAL: two training swords crossed on a wooden rack, evening light through a window]

And in the Ende, Hina gives birth. [ch.82-82] Licht and Tokikaze nearly draw swords outside the surgery room arguing over who holds the baby first — until Hina shuts them both down and calls in the man she asked to wait inside. [ch.82-82] It's **Schmelman**. He survived. He holds his great-grandchild and weeps — the baby has his eyes, his hair — and he says he hopes the peace lasts forever. [ch.82-82]

[VISUAL: an empty ledger page, the column where a number would go left blank]

[HOST] The child is born without a Count. No number. No worth assigned by a machine. Just a life. In a world that spent three hundred years reducing people to digits, that's the ending: a baby who is finally, simply, *a person.*
